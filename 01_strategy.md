## 1. Honest positioning

**What you sell:** AI workflow consulting for small and mid-sized businesses. You help non-technical owners use Claude, ChatGPT, and similar tools to save hours per week on writing, customer service, internal docs, and research.

**What you do not sell (yet):** custom-built AI agents in production, RAG pipelines plugged into client databases, anything a law firm would bet a case on. You skill up to those over the next 6–12 months and add them as paid offerings when you can deliver them safely.

**Why this order:** an intermediate hobbyist who launches by pitching law firms is one hallucinated citation away from getting a client sanctioned by the bar. The legal vertical is real and lucrative, but you enter it after you have (a) E&O insurance, (b) a documented eval process, (c) a few SMB case studies, and (d) ideally a paralegal or attorney as an advisor or partner.

**One-line pitch (working draft):**
> "I help small businesses in the South stop wasting hours on writing, emails, and admin by setting up AI workflows their team can actually use. Trained, documented, and supported — not a demo that breaks in a week."

## 3. Services + pricing (productized)

Productized = fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline. Avoid hourly until you have to. Four offerings, ordered from low-commit to high-commit:

| # | Service | Scope | Timeline | Price |
|---|---------|-------|----------|-------|
| 1 | **AI Readiness Audit** | 30-min interview + review of 3–5 workflows; written report with prioritized opportunities and tool recommendations | 1 week | **$750** flat |
| 2 | **Workflow Pack** | Build 3 documented prompts / Claude Projects / Custom GPTs for one chosen workflow (e.g. proposals, customer email, listing copy). Includes 1 hr training and a 30-day fix-it window | 2 weeks | **$2,500** flat |
| 3 | **Team Training Workshop** | Half-day in-person or remote training for up to 8 staff: tools overview, hands-on with their real work, internal usage policy template | 1 day | **$1,500** flat |
| 4 | **Retainer** | Monthly: 1 new workflow built per month, Slack/email support, monthly review call. Cap at 4 clients at a time | Monthly | **$2,000/mo** (3-month min) |

**Why these prices:** they're deliberately below "Bay Area expert" rates (~$200–400/hr) and above "Fiverr prompt seller" rates (~$50). They reflect a serious but accessible operator. Raise prices 20–30% after the first 3 paying clients and a testimonial in hand.

**Discovery call:** 20 minutes, free. No proposals over email without one.

## 4. Louisiana LLC formation — concrete steps

You are not legally a business until you file. Order matters.

1. **Pick the name** (above). Confirm uniqueness with the Secretary of State name search: search "Louisiana SOS business name search" — the official portal is GeauxBiz at the LA Secretary of State site.
2. **File Articles of Organization** (Form #365 or equivalent) for a Louisiana LLC. Filing fee is roughly **$100** (verify current fee on the SOS site — fees change). You can file online through GeauxBiz.
3. **Get an EIN** from the IRS — free, online, takes 10 minutes. URL: `irs.gov/ein`. Save the confirmation letter (CP 575).
4. **East Carroll Parish occupational license** — call the parish at the Lake Providence courthouse and ask what a single-member service LLC needs. Most LA parishes charge a small flat fee for consulting-type businesses.
5. **Open a business checking account** in the LLC's name. Bring: filed Articles, EIN letter, ID. Use a local bank if you want a relationship, or an online bank (Mercury, Relay) if you want better software.
6. **Bookkeeping** — set up either QuickBooks Online ($30/mo) or Wave (free). Track every business expense from day one.
7. **Sales tax** — Louisiana generally does *not* tax consulting services, but confirm with the LA Dept. of Revenue and a local CPA. If you ever sell prompt *packages* as digital products, that may change.

**Get a Louisiana attorney to review** your Operating Agreement and your client contract template (next section) before you sign your first client. Budget $500–$1,500 for a one-time engagement with a small-business attorney.

**Insurance — do not skip:**
- **Professional Liability / Errors & Omissions** ($1M aggregate) — Hiscox, Next Insurance, or Thimble will quote online. Expect $40–80/month for a solo consultant.
- **General Liability** if you ever do in-person workshops.
- **Cyber Liability** once you start handling any client data — required by most law firms before they'll sign.

## 5. Working with law firms — what changes

When you're ready for this vertical (phase 2), here's what's actually different:

- **You cannot give legal advice.** Even saying "this contract clause looks risky" is unauthorized practice of law in Louisiana. Stay in your lane: workflow, prompt, evaluation.
- **Confidentiality** — most firms will require a BAA-style agreement and proof that no client data is sent to consumer ChatGPT. You'll need to set them up on Claude for Work / ChatGPT Enterprise / Microsoft Copilot with the right data-retention settings.
- **Bar rules** — the ABA's Formal Opinion 512 (2024) and Louisiana's State Bar guidance both require attorneys to (a) understand the tool, (b) maintain confidentiality, (c) supervise AI work product, (d) disclose AI use where material, and (e) bill ethically. You don't enforce these — but your training and documentation should make compliance easy.
- **Eval discipline** — every prompt you ship to a firm needs documented test cases. Hallucinated citations are the *one* failure mode that ends careers. You need a process before you take this work.
- **Don't undersell** — legal hourly is high; your "Workflow Pack" for a firm should price at $5K–$10K, not $2.5K. Reflect the risk.

For now: don't pursue law firms. Mention them as a future vertical only.

## 6. Website — minimum viable

One-page site. Built first, optimized later. Sections in order:

1. **Hero** — name, one-line pitch, "Book a free 20-minute call" CTA
2. **What I do** — 3 services as cards (audit / workflow pack / training); price visible
3. **Who I work with** — list of business types (real estate, accounting, healthcare admin, marketing agencies, farms/ag); say plainly you're based in Louisiana and work remotely
4. **How it works** — 4 steps: call → audit → build → train (calm the buyer)
5. **About** — your photo, name, where you are, why you do this. Plain English, no AI hype
6. **FAQ** — 6 questions covering: confidentiality, what tools you use, what if it doesn't work, do I need to know AI, do you give refunds, how do you handle my data
7. **Footer + contact** — Calendly link or contact form

**Stack recommendation:** Next.js + Tailwind + shadcn/ui, deployed to Vercel free tier. Domain through Cloudflare (~$10/yr). Or if you want zero-code: Carrd ($19/yr) for the first version, replace later.

Defer: blog, case studies (write the first one after the first paying client), pricing page (price lives in the services section), team page.

## 9. Red flags to watch in yourself

- Pitching law firms before you're ready
- Saying yes to scope you can't deliver
- Hourly billing creep (always push to fixed-fee)
- Building before selling — don't write a single line of website code until you've talked to 5 potential customers and watched them react to the services list
- Imposter spiral — the bar is "do this better than the business owner could do it themselves in their spare time," not "be OpenAI"
